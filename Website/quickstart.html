Quick Start

Glossary - If you read anything, read this. Seriously. You'll be done in like five minutes.
Term
 Definition
Bucket Budgeting Bucket budeting is the concept of labeling buckets with expense names (i.e. 'dining', 'rent',  'phone', 'fun'), placing money in those buckets at regular intervals, and then paying expenses from those buckets as they occur. When a bucket is empty, you're done spending in that category until the bucket is refilled. The key concept in bucket budgeting is that you don't spend money you don't have.
Account
 An account represents money or debt. Checking, savings, and credit cards are all valid account types in ScratchTrack.
Expense
 An expense is any kind of outflow of money. An expense is paid from an account and attributed to a bucket.
Bucket
 A bucket is a place to assign money for upcoming expenses. You'll create buckets for different categories of expenses. Buckets are figurative and money stays in accounts until spent.
In-budget account
 An in-budget account is an account whose balance can be assigned to buckets.
Budgetable Dollars
 Budgetable dollars is the sum of the balances of  all in-budget accounts, minus any money already assigned to buckets. When money is assigned to a bucket, the money stays in the account and the account's balance doesn't change, but budgetable dollars decreases by that amount.
Folder
 Folders are used to organize buckets. I have three folders in my personal budget. The first two folders align with paycheck dates and are titled "15th" and "31st". Those folders contain buckets that I contribute to each month and I fill them when the associated paycheck arrives.The third folder is titled "out of band" and contains buckets that I don't fill on a regular basis.
Transaction
 A record of any cash flow

Walkthrough one: Fred sets up ScratchTrack
  • Fred stumbles upon this website and downloads ScratchTrack
  • Fred creates a checking account. He pays for things from this account, so he leaves it marked as 'in-budget'
  • Fred is paid $1500 twice a month, so he creates two folders called '15th paycheck' and '31st paycheck'
  • Fred sets the expense limit of each folder to $1500 since he knows he can't spend more than that

Walkthrough two: Fred gets paid
  • It's the 15th and Fred receives a $1500 paycheck
  • Fred deposits it into his in-budget checking account by clicking the deposit icon in the account list. Fred could've also right clicked on the account and selected deposit

  • Fred records the deposit in ScratchTrack
    • ScratchTrack reports that 'checking' has a balance of $1500
    • ScratchTrack reports that there are $1500 budgetable dollars

Walkthrough three: Fred assigns money to buckets

It's the 15th. Fred has been paid. Fred is ready to assign money to the buckets in the '15th' folder. Fred has two ways to fill his buckets.

Option one - fill all the buckets in the folder
If Fred clicks the 'Assign Money' icon next to the folder (or selects that option after right clicking on the folder), Fred can fill all the buckets in the 15th folder in one shot. There are two ways to fill. The first option adds the budgeted amount to each bucket. If a bucket already has money in it, it will be over-funded. This is useful if you're saving for a long-term expense, like a car insurance that's paid twice a year. The other option sets the buckets to their budgeted amount.

If dining, which has a budget of $200, already has $100 in it, option one will fill the bucket to $300, and option two will fill the bucket to $200.


Option two - fill buckets individually
If Fred clicks the 'Assign Money' icon next to the bucket (or right clicks), Fred can fill an bucket individually.


Fred has elected to fill all his buckets to their budgeted amounts


...and the state of the budget now indicates that all budgetable dollars have been assigned to buckets

Walkthrough four: Fred dines at McDonald's

Fred is feeling rich and treats himself to McDonald's. He spends $15.37. He records the transaction in ScratchTrack by clicking the 'Record Expense' icon next to the bucket. He could've also right clicked the bucket.



ScratchTrack shows Fred his total dining expenses and remaining funds

Walkthrough five: Fred gets a credit card

Fred has learned that he can get 5% back on his Amazon purchases if he pays with an Amazon credit card. This sounds good to Fred (and I agree!) so he gets one.


Fred adds the credit card account the same was as he added his other accounts, but this time he checks 'Credit Card Account'

When he does this, a new folder and bucket is created. These items are not editable.


When Fred pays for something with his Amazon credit card, he records it in the associated bucket. Let's say he's ordered ramen noodles from Amazon.


Fred has recorded the expense in the 'groceries' bucket and has selected his Amazon credit card as the payment account.

Two things have happened:
  1. The balance of the 'groceries' bucket has decreased by the expense amount of $27.50
  2. The balance of the 'Pay Amazon CC' bucket has increased by the expense amount of $27.50
Why does ScratchTrack do this? Credit cards are a buy now, pay later model. Deducting from the 'groceries' bucket immediately is important. Fred needs to know how much he has left to spend. Since we're paying later, we still have the money in our checking account, but we want to make sure it's not used for something else. That's where the 'Pay CC' envelopes come in.

Walkthrough five: Fred's credit card bill is due

The time has come for Fred to pay his credit card bill. He hasn't made any additional credit card purchases, so the bill is $27.50. Fred clicks the 'Pay Credit Card' icon next to the Amazon account. He also could've right clicked to get there. He sets the payment amount $27.50.



After payment, the checking balance is reduced, and the credit card balance is cleared.
