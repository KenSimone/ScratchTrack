Category-based Budgeting		 All these budgeting schemes are fundamentally the same and ScratchTrack follows the same flow. Expenses are grouped into categories (or buckets, or envelopes). Funds are assigned to categories. Expenses are tracked against categories. When a category is out of funds, spending in that category stops until more funds are added. The first rule of ScratchTrack is that you don't talk about ScratchTrack, but the second rule is that you don't spend money you don't have.
Category
 Categories provide an organized view of the funds in accounts. You'll create categories for different types of expenses (rent, groceries, dining out, utilities, etcetera) and assign money to them. Understanding categories is important so we'll do a quick walkthrough:
  1. Fred has a checking account with a $500 balance, all of which is assignable to categories
  2. Fred assigns $200 to his 'dining out' category and $200 to his 'furry fetish' category
  3. Fred's checking balance is still $500, but only $100 is assignable since $400 was assigned in step two
  4. Fred spends $15 at McDonalds and $85 on a realistic squirrel tail
  5. Fred now has $400 in his account, $185 is assigned to 'dining out', $115 is assigned 'furry fetish', and $100 is available to assign to other categories
There are two main take-aways here: The first is that furry accessories are expensive and your money is probably better spent elsewhere. The second is that categories are just a view of the money in accounts. Assigning funds to a category doesn't move money. It just marks it for a specific purpose.
Account
 In ScratchTrack, unspent funds are always in an account. Funds can be assigned to categories, but again, categories are just a view of the money in the accounts. Money stays in account(s) until spent. 
Expense An expense is any kind of outflow of money. An expense is paid from an account and attributed to a category.
In-budget account
 An in-budget account is an account whose balance can be assigned to categories. If you have a checking account with $200 and another checking account with $50, and both are in-budget, you'll have $250 you can assign to categories.
Budgetable Dollars
 Budgetable dollars is the sum of the balances of  all in-budget accounts, minus any money already assigned to categories. When money is assigned to a category, the money stays in the account and the account's balance doesn't change, but budgetable dollars decreases by that amount.
Folder
 Folders are used to organize categories. I have three folders in my personal budget. The first two folders align with paycheck dates and are titled "15th" and "31st". Those folders contain categories that I contribute to each month and I fill them when the associated paycheck arrives.The third folder is titled "out of band" and contains categories that I don't fill on a regular basis.
Transaction
 A record of any cash flow

ScratchTrack User Interface
ScratchTrack is a single screen application with four main areas
  • The accounts list displays accounts and balances
  • The budget info window displays budget status
  • The folders and categories tree displays folders and categories
  • The transaction list displays transactions
Most items in ScratchTrack are editable with a double click, and more options are available by right clicking. Additionally, accounts, folders, and categories have action icons to quickly perform operations.


Workflow walkthroughs
Walkthrough one: Fred sets up ScratchTrack
  • Fred stumbles upon this website and downloads ScratchTrack
  • Fred creates a checking account. He pays for things from this account, so he leaves it marked as 'in-budget'
  • Fred is paid $1500 twice a month, so he creates two folders called '15th paycheck' and '31st paycheck'
  • Fred sets the expense limit of each folder to $1500 since he knows he can't spend more than that


Walkthrough two: Fred gets paid
  • It's the 15th and Fred receives a $1500 paycheck
  • Fred deposits it into his in-budget checking account by clicking the deposit icon in the account list. Fred could've also right clicked on the account and selected deposit

Walkthrough three: Fred creates categories

Fred has accounts and folders, but he doesn't have a budget. Fred will make a best guess at dividing up his paycheck

Walkthrough six: Fred gets a credit card

Fred has learned that he can get 5% back on his Amazon purchases if he pays with an Amazon credit card. This sounds good to Fred (and I agree!) so he gets one.



Walkthrough five: Fred's credit card bill is due

The time has come for Fred to pay his credit card bill. He hasn't made any additional credit card purchases, so the bill is $27.50. Fred clicks the 'Pay Credit Card' icon next to the Amazon account. He also could've right clicked to get there. He sets the payment amount $27.50.



After payment, the checking balance is reduced, and the credit card balance is cleared.
