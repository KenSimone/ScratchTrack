ScratchTrack 0.5

Quick Start

Glossary - If you read anything, read this. Seriously. You'll be done in like five minutes.
Term
 Definition
Envelope (or bucket) budgeting
 In envelope budgeting, expenses are grouped into categories (envelopes) and funds are assigned to those categories. Expenses are tracked against categories. When the money assigned to a category is spent, spending in that category stops until more funds are added.

Key concept: You don't spend money you don't have.
Categories
 Categories provide an organized view of the funds in accounts. You'll create categories for different types of expenses (rent, groceries, dining out, utilities, etcetera) and assign money to them. Understanding categories is important so let's do a quick walkthrough:
  1. Fred has a checking account with a $500 balance
  2. Fred assigns $200 to his 'dining out' category and $200 to his 'groceries' category
  3. Fred's checking balance is still $500, but he can only assign $100 to other categories since he assigned $400 of his $500 in step two
  4. Fred spends $15 at McDonalds and $85 on groceries
  5. Fred now has $400 in his checking account
    1. $100 has been spent
    2. $185 is assigned to the 'dining out' category
    3. $115 is assigned to the 'groceries' category
    4. $100 is still available to assign to other categories
Key concept: Categories are just views of the money in accounts. Assigning funds to a category doesn't move money. It just marks it for a specific purpose.
Accounts
 In ScratchTrack, unspent funds are always in an account. Funds can be assigned to categories, but categories are just a view of the money in the accounts.

Key concept: Money stays in accounts until spent. 
In-budget account
 An in-budget account is an account whose balance can be assigned to categories. Accounts that you typically spend from (i.e. checking accounts) are usually in-budget. You can pay for things from off-budget accounts, but off-budget balances aren't used to fund the monthly budget.
Budgetable Dollars
 Budgetable (or assignable) dollars is the sum of in-budget account balances minus funds already assigned to categories. When money is assigned to a category, the money stays in the account and the account's balance doesn't change, but budgetable dollars decreases by that amount.
Folder
 Folders are used to organize categories. I have three folders in my personal budget. The first two folders align with paycheck dates and are titled "15th" and "31st". Those folders contain categories that I contribute to each month. I refill them when the associated paycheck arrives.The third folder is titled "out of band" and contains categories that I don't fill on a regular basis.

Folders can optionally have spending limits. If you budget or spend over the limit ScratchTrack warns you.


ScratchTrack User Interface
ScratchTrack's single window interface has four main areas
  • The accounts list displays accounts and balances
  • The budget info window displays budget status
  • The folders and categories tree displays folders and categories
  • The transaction list displays transactions
Most items in ScratchTrack are editable with a double click, and more options are available by right clicking.


Workflow walk-throughs
Walk-through one: Fred sets up ScratchTrack
  • Fred adds a checking account. He pays for things from this account so he leaves it marked as 'in-budget'
  • Fred is paid $1500 twice a month, so he creates two folders called '15th paycheck' and '31st paycheck'
  • Fred sets the expense limit of each folder to $1500 since he knows he can't spend more than that


Walk-through two: Fred gets paid
  • It's the 15th and Fred receives his $1500 paycheck
  • Fred deposits it into his in-budget checking account by clicking the deposit icon in the account list.
  • Note the entry in the tre transaction list after the deposit. All transactions are placed in this list and can be edited

Walk-through three: Fred creates categories
  • Fred has accounts and folders but he doesn't have a budget. Fred makes a best guess at dividing up his paycheck
  • The informtion column shows much room there is in the folder for additonal categories
  • When Fred over-budgets the '15th paycheck' folder ScratchTrack displays a warning
  • Pro tip: When you create or edit a category you can set transaction defaults. These defaults will be pre-populated when you add a new transaction in that category.

Walk-through four: Fred assigns funds to categories
  • Fred has accounts and categories and is now ready to fund his monthly expenses
  • Fred can apply funds to individual categories or to all categories in a folder
  • Fred can choose to 'top off' categories to their budgeted amount
    • This option funds categories to their budgeted amount
    • If the 'Dining' category has a budget of $200 and has $100 in it, this options adds $100, funding the category to $200
  • Fred can choose to assign the fully budgeted amount to categories
    • This option adds the full budget amount to a category regrdless of funds already in it
    • This option is useful for long term savings goals
    • If the 'Dining' category has a budget of $200 and has $100 in it, this options adds $200, funding the category to $300
  • Fred can choose to assign arbitrary amounts to indiidual categories

Walk-through  five: Fred Spends
  • Now that Fred's categories are funded, he can track expenses against them
  • Expenses are paid from accounts, and tracked against categories
  • When an expense is recorded, the account balance and the category balance is deducted by the expense amount
    • Remember: Categories are just views of the funds in accounts

Congratulations! At this point, you know the general worklfow, but there's one more topic to cover: Credit Cards.
Walk-through six: Fred gets a credit card

Fred has learned that he can get 5% back on his Amazon purchases if he pays with an Amazon credit card. This sounds good to Fred (and I agree!) so he gets one.
  • When Fred adds a credit card account, a category titled 'Credit card payment' is automatically created
  • When an expense is record against the credit card money moves from the original expense category into the 'Credit card payment' category
  • Credit cards are paid from the account list
  • Like any expense, credit cards are paid from an account (i.e. checking), and tracked against a category (in this case the 'Credit card payment' category).
