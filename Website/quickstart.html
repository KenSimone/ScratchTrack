ScratchTrack 0.5

Quick Start

Glossary - If you read anything, read this. Seriously. You'll be done in like five minutes.
Term
 Definition
Bucket Budgeting Bucket budeting is the concept of labeling buckets with expense names (i.e. 'dining', 'rent',  'phone', 'fun'), placing money in those buckets at regular intervals, and then paying expenses from those buckets as they occur. When an bucket is empty, you're done spending in that category until the bucket is refilled. The key concept in bucket budgeting is that you don't spend money you don't have.
Account
 An account represents money or debt. Checking, savings, and credit cards are all valid account types in ScratchTrack.
Expense
 An expense is any kind of outflow of money. An expense is paid from an account and attributed to an bucket.
Bucket
 An bucket is a place to assign money for upcoming expenses. You'll create buckets for different categories of expenses. Buckets are figurative and money stays in accounts until spent.
In-budget account
 An in-budget account is an account whose balance can be assigned to buckets.
Budgetable Dollars
 Budgetable dollars is the sum of the balances of  all in-budget accounts, minus any money already assigned to buckets. When money is assigned to a bucket, the money stays in the account and the account's balance doesn't change, but budgetable dollars decreases by that amount.
Folder
 Folders are used to organize buckets. I have three folders in my personal budget. The first two folders align with paycheck dates and are titled "15th" and "31st". Those folders contain buckets that I contribute to each month and I fill them when the associated paycheck arrives.The third folder is titled "out of band" and contains buckets that I don't fill on a regular basis.
Transaction
 A record of any cash flow

Workflow walkthrough  - You should probably read this too
Example one: Fred gets paid and eats out
  • Fred receives a $500 paycheck
  • Fred deposits it into his empty in-budget checking account
  • Fred records the deposit in ScratchTrack
    • ScratchTrack reports that 'checking' has a balance of $500
    • ScratchTrack reports that there are $500 budgetable dollars
  • Fred assigns $200 to the 'dining out' bucket
    • ScratchTrack reports that 'checking' has a balance of $500
    • ScratchTrack reports that there are $300 budgetable dollars
    • ScratchTrack reports that the 'dining out' bucket has $200 spendable dollars
  • Fred spends $15 from his checking account at McDonald's and records it in ScratchTrack
    • ScratchTrack reports that 'checking' has a balance of $485
    • ScratchTrack reports that there are $300 budgetable dollars
    • ScratchTrack reports that the 'dining out' bucket has $185 spendable dollars
The key take away is that expenses are tracked through buckets, but money stays in its account until spent

Setting things up - You can probably stumble around the app without reading this, but it'll help
Adding Accounts
  1. Click 'Add Account...'
  2. Enter a name for the account.
  3. Enter the initial balance of the account
If an account typically has a positive balance and is used to pay for things, leave "Contributes to monthly budget" checked.

Accounts that contribute to the budget can have their balance assigned to buckets (future expenses). We'll cover buckets in a bit.

Add as many accounts as you like.


Adding Credit Card Accounts
ScratchTrack supports credit card accounts

If a credit card account has a balance, it's typically negative (money is owed).

In ScatchTrack you can pay for items with credit cards, but credit cards never contribute to the monthly budget since they don't have a positive balance.
Adding Folders
Folders organize buckets.

You can create folders any way you like, but creating folders based on income events works well

In this example we're paid on the 15th and the 31st and so we're creating folders to match.

Folders have expense limits. This helps with budget planning. In this example the expense limit is set to the usual paycheck amount. As we organize the budget, we can track how much of this limit we've consumed.

There's also a folder titled "Credit Cards". This is automatically created when you add a credit card account. More on that later.
Adding Buckets
Buckets are the heart and soul of the workflow.

The idea is to assign dollars to buckets before money is spent. When an expense is incurred, the expense is paid from an account with dollars assigned to an bucket for that expense type.

In this example, we're setting up an bucket for our car payment.

Budgeting our car payment is easy because we know exactly how much we'll need each month. In this case it's $237.84.

The bottom half of this dialog box sets default transaction settings. You don't have to set any defaults, and you can change from the defaults when you enter a transaction.


Folder expense limits
Now that we have buckets in our folder, we can see how our budget lines up with our folder limit.

Remember, we set the '15th paycheck' folder limit to our paycheck amount of $2,138.47.

So far, we've budgeted $787.84 in that folder and we haven't recorded any expenses.

Dollars available for buckets
Now that we've got accounts, folders, and buckets, we can start assigning dollars to buckets.

In this example, we have $534.22 available to assign to buckets.

The $5,000 in savings is not included because I unchecked "Contributes to monthly budget" when adding that account. I do not want to use money from savings to pay for things.
Assign dollars to an bucket
We've clicked the 'assign money to bucket' icon to bring up the bucket funding dialog box for the 'Car Payment' bucket. Right clicking the bucket offers the same options.

Note the three options.

The first option ADDS the budgeted amount to any existing amount in the bucket. This is useful for saving towards a long-term goal. An example might be a car insurance policy, where you contribute monthly, but pay twice a year.

The second option SETS the bucket to the budgetd amount. If we already had $100 in this bucket, this option would add $137.84 to bring the bucket's balance to the budgeted amount.

The last option sets the bucket's balance to any amount.

The second screen shot shows the funded balance.
