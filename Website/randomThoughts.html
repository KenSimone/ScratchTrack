Random thoughts on budgeting

Chase dollars before pennies
The internet is filled with articles that tell you to cut out trips to Starbucks. I don't disagree, but if trips to Starbucks are causing financial stress, you may want to look at bigger expenses first. A well-equipped ten year old Honda Civic will get you around fine for less than $10K. If you're paying a fortune to live in an upscale area, stepping down a notch might make sense.

Chase quarters and dimes next
There's usually room to trim in the next tier of expenses too. Cell, internet, auto and home insurance can all usually benefit from a yearly review. General corporate strategy seems to be to rip off existing customers to offer deals to new customers. Always be the new customer. Brand loyalty is dead. I perpetually cycle between Geico, StateFarm, Allstate and Progressive.

Don't forget about the nickels and pennies either
This is where we circle back to Starbucks, Netflix, Hulu, Spotify, and Chick-Fil-A. Cut back if you have to.

Perpetual fees are freaking evil
As part of the "rip off existing customers" corporate strategy, corporations are making an effort to turn everything into a perpetual fee. Reject that where possible. If there's no additional value being added after the initial purchase there shouldn't be a recurring cost. The whole reason I wrote ScratchTrack was because this happened to budgeting software. Anyway, review your recurring expenses. Are they worth it?

You should use credit cards
Credit cards offer an insane layer of fraud protection and you get paid to use them. Be responsible. Use ScratchTrack to track your spending to ensure you can pay it off every month. NerdWallet rates the best reward cards. Find one that suites you and use it responsibly.
